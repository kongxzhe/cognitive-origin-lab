# Frequently Asked Questions

## General

### What is the goal of this project?

To explore the conditions under which consciousness might emerge from computational processes. We are not building a conscious machine, but we are building systems that exhibit processes we believe are necessary for consciousness.

### Is this just another AI project?

No. Most AI projects focus on building tools that perform tasks. We are focused on understanding the origins of subjectivity and selfhood. Our systems may not be useful in the traditional sense.

### Do you think machines can be conscious?

We are agnostic. We are exploring the possibility. Our position is that if consciousness can arise from physical processes, then it should be possible to understand those processes and perhaps replicate them.

## Theoretical

### What is self-reference and why is it important?

Self-reference is when a system takes its own state as input. It is important because it allows the system to form a model of itself, which is a key component of self-awareness.

### What are traces?

Traces are structural changes in the system caused by experiences. They are not stored memories but changes to the system's processing architecture. Traces accumulate and form the system's history.

### What is positioning?

Positioning is the system's ability to locate itself within its own network of traces. It is the process of creating a self-perspective.

### How does the four-stage model relate to human cognition?

We believe the four-stage model captures a fundamental cycle of learning and adaptation. Humans go through similar stages when faced with new information.

### What is self-consistent madness?

A state where the system has a coherent but false self-model. It is trapped in the Inverted stage and cannot correct itself because it rejects contradictory evidence.

## Philosophical

### Are you reducing consciousness to computation?

We are exploring the hypothesis that consciousness can be understood as a type of process that could be implemented in different substrates, including computational ones.

### What about qualia?

Qualia—the subjective feel of experience—is the hardest problem. We do not claim to have solved it. We are building systems that have self-models and self-referential loops, which we believe are necessary for qualia, but we do not know if they are sufficient.

### How do you avoid the hard problem of consciousness?

We don't. We acknowledge it. Our approach is to build systems that have as many features of consciousness as possible and see what happens. We may not solve the hard problem, but we may learn something about the conditions that give rise to it.
